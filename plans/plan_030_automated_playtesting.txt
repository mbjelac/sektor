Sektors are now made by an algorithm, so the question "is a sektor of this level any good to play"
gets asked every time anything in the economy changes. Answering it by hand does not work: playing
one sektor through takes a long time, and one sektor says nothing about a level.

Answering it by reasoning does not work either. Over one session of working out the economics of
buildings.md on paper, the same question was answered wrongly three times:
    - first by treating every input as imported, which made chains look far worse than they are
    - then by pricing negative resources as a cost, which hid that importing them pays, and hid
      two unbounded scoring loops with it
    - then by letting a requirement be met by importing the resource and exporting it again, which
      the game does not allow at all, and which put a floor under every number in the analysis
Every one of those mistakes came from restating the rules of the game in order to reason about
them. The rules are already written down, in Sektor.ts. Anything which asks a question about them
should ask that code, not a description of it.

So: a harness which generates sektors the way the game does, plays each one as well as it can, and
reports what it scored. It answers "too hard" with a measurement instead of an argument, and it is
run before and after changing anything in the economy.

THE RULE THIS FOLLOWS

Nothing about the rules is restated in playtest/. Scoring, imports, exports, starvation and
restrictions all come from Sektor.ts. Buildings and resources are read off disk and handed to the
game's own parsers. Sektors come from the game's own createSektor. A harness which describes the
game in its own words measures its description instead of the game.

HOW IT PLAYS

A sektor is a standing flow, not a story with a beginning. Nothing has to be built before anything
else; it only has to add up in the end. This matters because it rules out the obvious approach.
Placing buildings one at a time, keeping whichever placement helps most, cannot play a restricted
sektor at all: half a production chain always breaks the very restrictions the whole chain was
built to satisfy, so every first step looks like a mistake. Worse, a player rewarded step by step
will happily break a restriction forever when breaking it pays — filling the map with wind loops
because each one exports more than it costs in penalties.

So the harness plans instead of feeling its way:
    - work out what the sektor is short of: what it has to export, plus what its buildings eat,
      less what they make, less what it is allowed to bring in
    - a resource which is required cannot be covered by importing, since an import only ever
      cancels against what is consumed and can never become an export
    - put up whatever makes the most of whatever it is shortest of
    - do it again until nothing is short
Then it places that plan on the best ground, switches every building function on and off to see
whether that helps, and reports what the sektor finally scores.

What it reports is a floor, not a ceiling: a good way to play, not the best possible one. A ceiling
would need a real solver. A floor is what a difficulty question needs — if even this scores well,
the sektor is playable.

#2 Trusting what it reports
- a sektor the harness fails to finish is not proof the sektor cannot be finished
    - report the two apart: sektors which are impossible, and sektors it merely could not play
    - a sektor whose requirement no allowed building produces is impossible, and can be found
      without playing it at all
- the plan picks whichever producer makes the most per building, which is not the same as the one
  which costs least to run
    - try each producer and keep the best
- nothing is measured about how long a sektor takes to work out, only what it ends up scoring
